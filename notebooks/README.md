## Analysed population

`_common.load_dataset` drops 54 race-years (event_ids) of the merged file before any analysis, with the identifiers listed and commented in `_common.py`; `descriptives.txt` section 0 persists the counts by reason and event type, and `excluded_race_years.csv` one row per excluded race-year:

- **14 duplicated race-years** (`DUPLICATE_RACE_YEARS`): a second copy of a race already in the file. Thirteen are pairs inside the secondary source, which lists the race under two names (70.3 Cancun = Cozumel 2012; Vineman = Santa Rosa 2014–2016; Buenos Aires = South American Championship 2016–2019; Alagoas = Maceió 2017; Virginia = Virginia's Blue Ridge 2019; "70.3 Mar del Plata" = Ironman Argentina 2017–2019), and one is the official "2019 IRONMAN World Championship Kukui Division", a subset of the secondary source's complete copy of that race. The shares of shared finishers (bib, overall, swim and cycling times) are recomputed at every run (`duplicate_overlap`, `descriptives.txt` §0), and step 11 checks the Methods bound of at least 99.8% on the dropped copies. The copy kept is the more complete one: (1) a subset is dropped; (2) a copy whose event type contradicts its finish times is dropped (full-distance times under a 70.3 name); (3) otherwise the copy with more records is kept, then the one with more finishers, then the one with more recorded split times; (4) between copies that tie on all of these, the copy named as the series is named in its other years. The kept copies of 70.3 Vineman 2014–2016 and 70.3 Buenos Aires 2017–2019 carry no transition splits, which the dropped copies have.
- **29 events in non-standard formats** (`NONSTANDARD_FORMAT_EVENTS`, identified by the event-name rules in `NONSTANDARD_FORMAT_RULES`; `load_dataset` fails if the rules and the list disagree): 10 Olympic-distance "5150" races recorded as IRONMAN® events, 11 aquabike races (ten of them record every starter as DNF; one secondary-source event is spelled "Aqaubike"), 7 bike-run races and the "2023 IRONMAN Kalmar-Sweden DO-NOT-USE (see notes)" event.
- **5 races of another brand** (`NON_IRONMAN_EVENTS`, identified by the event-name rules in `NON_IRONMAN_BRANDS`; `load_dataset` fails if the rules and the list disagree): races of another brand recorded as IRONMAN® events — the secondary source's Challenge Family races Challenge Copenhagen 2010–2012 and Challenge Weymouth 2014–2015 (5,754 starters, 893 DNFs). The rules also cover Rev3, HITS, Xterra, Ultraman, Escape and Oceanman, none of which occurs in either source.
- **6 events stopped or recorded wholesale as DNF** (`STOPPED_EDITIONS`): events of either source whose in-race DNFs are at least 40% of their starters — 2011 IRONMAN 70.3 Mallorca (every starter an in-race DNF: 1,628 of the 1,631 with a swim split and 1,628 with a T1 split (1,625 with both), 1,535 with a cycling split, 1,396 with a T2 split, none with a run split), 2013 IRONMAN 70.3 Switzerland (every starter an in-race DNF: all 1,188 with a swim split, 1,084 also with a T1 split, 1 with a cycling split, none with a T2 or run split), 2019 IRONMAN 70.3 Texas (82.8%; 1,581 of its 1,850 in-race DNFs at the cycling-completion distance, 1,577 of them with a T2 split, none beyond it), Ironman Japan 2007 (52.4%; none with a run split, no transition split recorded for the race), Ironman 70.3 Brazil 2008 (46.1%; every in-race DNF with a swim split, 97% with a cycling split, none with a run split, no transition split recorded for the race) and Ironman 70.3 Superfrog 2014 (4 of 4 starters, each with a run split but no finish time). The threshold lies in the gap between the 99.5th percentile of the events and the lowest listed event. The rule is evaluated on the events left after the three exclusions above; `load_dataset` fails unless it flags exactly these events. `descriptives.txt` section 0 persists the distribution that justifies the threshold, for all events and for each source: on both sources the next-highest event below it is 35.0% (Ironman Japan 2006) and the 99.5th percentile 26.9% (official source alone: 26.7% and 22.8%); `excluded_race_years.csv` gives the in-race DNFs of each excluded race-year. `dropout_hazard.py` repeats the main analysis with these events kept (sensitivity `including_stopped`; only the official ones carry distances).

Excluded: 46,174 records, 40,247 starters, 7,617 DNFs. Analysed: 3,586,149 records in 2,127 race-years (2,126 with at least one starter).

Events held over a shortened course are kept (`shortened_editions`, listed in `descriptives.txt` section 0 and `shortened_editions.csv`): an official-source event with its own course (below) and a modal finisher total distance below 90% of nominal, or an event whose finishers' median overall time is below 75% of the typical median of its event type (the median of the event medians, both sources), or whose name declares a partial course. The 33 official events held without a swim are reported apart and not counted as shortened.

## Course positions

`total_distance_km` is the position of the last timing mat crossed. Each official-source event has its own swim exit, cycling completion and finish: the modal swim, swim + cycling and total distances of its finishers (`event_course_table`; `event_course.csv`). Every boundary is compared with the same tolerance, equality at two decimals (`km_past`). The nominal positions (3.86 / 184.11 / 226.31 km and 1.93 / 92.05 / 113.15 km) are used when the finishers carry no distance (basis `nominal_no_distance`; these 135 events hold no DNF with a distance); a modal run distance above 125% of nominal is replaced by the nominal run length. `dropout_point.txt` section 3 reports how many events deviate from the nominal positions and the most common own values.

**Distances not used (course rule).** When the finishers' distances do not form a usable course — their modal total is not the sum of the modal swim, cycling and run distances, or is 0 (basis `nominal_incoherent`), or lies below 90% of nominal although the finish times are typical, i.e. distances recorded on another scale such as miles or a 70.3 scale (basis `nominal_scale`) — the DNFs' distances of that event cannot be placed on any course either. `_common.attach_course` sets `total_distance_km` of every record of these events to missing (`UNUSABLE_DISTANCE_BASES`), so every distance-based analysis treats them as records without a distance: the distance classification, `run_position`, the cross-tabulation, the hazard, the finish-time DNF distance checks, the professional distance analysis (`pro_curation.txt` §3) and Figure 3. Eight events (six IRONMAN® 70.3 of 2018, 2018 IRONMAN Australia and 2019 IRONMAN Lake Placid) hold 1,176 DNF records with a recorded distance (978 in-race). `dropout_point.txt` section 3 lists them and gives the coverage of the field both as recorded (41.4% of all DNFs) and after the rule (40.8%).

The distance classes (`infer_dropout_from_distance`) are "no timing mat crossed" (distance 0, the field's default, compatible with withdrawal during the swim), swim (at or before the swim exit), cycling (beyond it, at or before the cycling completion) and run (beyond the cycling completion). Run-segment DNFs (`run_position`, identical in `dropout_point.py` section 5 and `dropout_hazard.py`): in-race DNFs beyond the cycling completion and before the finish distance, at their running distance, plus — under the T2 rule of the main hazard analysis — in-race DNFs exactly at the cycling-completion distance whose T2 split is > 0 and at most the **T2 duration limit** of their event type (`at_t2`; median T2 split 8.7 and 5.8 min): they crossed the timing mat at the transition exit and entered the run at km 0. The limit (`_common.t2_limits`, `T2_LIMIT_QUANTILE`) is the 99th percentile of the T2 splits of the official-source finishers with a T2 split > 0, computed on the loaded data: 1,204 s (20.07 min) in IRONMAN®, 754 s (12.57 min) in IRONMAN® 70.3. A DNF at that distance with a longer T2 split (`at_t2_long`; median 34.2 and 23.7 min) or without a T2 split (`at_no_t2`) withdrew in the transition and is not modelled in the main analysis. Sensitivity analyses (`RUN_RULES`): `strict` counts none of the DNFs at the cycling-completion distance; `t2_no_limit` counts every one with a T2 split (`at_t2` + `at_t2_long`). Records at the finish distance are not run DNFs (their interval would have zero width). `dropout_point.txt` section 5 and `dropout_point_t2.csv` persist the limits, the counts of each class and the median T2 splits; section 5 also gives the distribution of the last run checkpoint restricted to the events with at least one run DNF beyond km 0 (in the others every run DNF is at km 0), and, by season, the share of the run DNFs of the main rule recorded at km 0 (`dropout_point_by_season.csv`, with numerators and denominators).

## Race location of secondary-source races

The race location of secondary-source (aggregator) races is not read from the `region` field of `race_metadata.csv`. That field is IRONMAN's commercial region, not a geographic continent, and it carries coding errors: 70.3 Dubai 2016–2019 and 2022 and 70.3 Tiberias (Israel) 2021–2022 coded Africa; IRONMAN Israel 2022 coded Europe; 70.3 Costa Rica 2017–2019 and 70.3 Riviera Nayarit (Mexico) 2024–2025 coded South America; 70.3 Saipan 2017 coded Asia; and, among races that are not in the merged file, 70.3 Victoria (Canada) coded Australia, the 2025 women's IRONMAN World Championship in Kona coded Europe, 70.3 Bahrain, Oman, Salalah and Muscat races coded Africa, and Mexican, Central American and Caribbean 70.3 races coded South America.

One rule applies to both sources: each race is mapped to the country that hosts it (secondary source: the series, i.e. the race name without its year, mapped by hand; World Championships by year), and the host country to a continent by `CONTINENT_MAP`, so a race gets the continent of an athlete from the same country (Israel, the Gulf states and Turkey = Asia; Mexico, Central America and the Caribbean = North America; US states and territories, including Hawaii and Saipan, = North America). The official series follow the same rule, e.g. `im703-turkey` (Turkey = Asia) and `im703-san-juan`, whose events from 2022 are held in San Juan, Argentina (South America; the San Juan race in Puerto Rico is `im703-puerto-rico`). `build_race_location_table()` asserts that every host country is in `CONTINENT_MAP` and that every series matched across the two sources by its normalised name, and every series across its years, receives a single host country (World Championships excepted). A single continent per country needs no test: the continent is `CONTINENT_MAP` of the country, so it holds by construction. `athlete_vs_venue.txt` §1 reports how many secondary-source starters the host-country rule places on another continent than the region field; the region field is read only for that count.

The next version of the companion dataset will carry the same correction.

## Key Methodological Decisions

Documented in `_common.py` and in the docstrings of the scripts:

1. **Zero-handling:** the official platform fills absent splits with 0 for DNFs — treated as missing (split > 0 required). Supporting shares persisted in `dropout_point.txt` §1 by source.
2. **Finish-time and in-race DNFs:** a finish-time DNF is a DNF with a recorded overall finish time (`overall_sec > 0`); an in-race DNF is a DNF without one (`overall_sec` missing or 0). The groups are named by what the record shows, not by a presumed reason: the official source flags finish-time DNFs as DNF and leaves them unranked (`rank_overall` placeholder 99999) while recording a complete finish time, and most of their times lie below the typical cut-off (17.0 h IRONMAN®, 8.5 h IRONMAN® 70.3). In-race DNFs are the outcome of the regression and of the hazard. Description of the finish-time group (overall time vs the typical cut-off, ranking, concentration by event) and its internal consistency (sum of splits vs overall; distance vs the event's modal finisher distance, two criteria: at or above it, and within 3 km of it on either side) persisted in `dropout_point.txt` §1.
3. **Dropout localization:**
   - Method 1 (distance): `total_distance_km` → "no timing mat crossed" + 3 segments (swim/bike/run) against each event's own course positions (*Course positions*), in-race DNFs only (finish-time DNFs are a separate category, as in the cross-tabulation and the hazard). The field is the position of the **last timing mat crossed**, not a continuous distance. The distances of the events without a usable course are not used (course rule, *Course positions*). The coverage statistic of the field (share of DNFs carrying a distance value) stays defined on all DNFs, finish-time DNFs included, as recorded and after the course rule (`dropout_point.txt` §3); its coverage among the official-source in-race DNFs is also given by season (`dropout_point_by_season.csv`). The segment shares are persisted in `dropout_point_segments.csv`: the distance classification and, as variant `t2_rule`, the segments of the hazard's run-DNF definition, derived from `run_class` (run = T2-completers within the duration limit at km 0 + DNFs beyond the cycling completion and before the finish distance; DNFs at or beyond the finish distance are separate categories; every other DNF keeps its distance class), so its run share is the run DNFs of the hazard over the in-race DNFs with a distance used.
   - Method 2 (splits): last segment with split > 0 → "last completed segment" (not "dropout point").
   - Method 3 (cross-tabulation) on records with both; label agreement is capped by construction (T1/T2 split labels cannot match swim/bike/run distance labels).
   - **Hazard:** interval-censored hazard per km, events with ≥ 30 run-segment DNFs under the analysis' own rule, at-risk = finishers + finish-time DNFs + run DNFs at or beyond the mat, cluster bootstrap over events (1,000 reps, seed 42, a fresh generator per bootstrap; contrasts based on fewer than 10 events with the start of the run observed flagged as underpowered). Run DNFs as in *Course positions*: `km_run = round(total_distance_km − event's cycling completion, 2)`; main analysis with the T2 rule (DNFs at `km_run = 0` with a T2 split within the duration limit enter at km 0); sensitivities `strict` (no DNF at the cycling-completion distance enters the run), `t2_no_limit` (the T2 rule without the duration limit) and `including_stopped` (the stopped or wholesale-coded events kept), each with its between, within and curve contrasts, plus the cohort, period, at-risk-set and start-of-the-run sensitivities (same run-DNF definition in `dropout_point.py` §5). The start of the run is observed in an event at km 0 (whenever at least one T2-completer withdrew there) or at a checkpoint within 1 km of the cycling completion. Events without a checkpoint at or beyond the threshold of the start of the run (1 km; 0.5 or 1.5 km in those sensitivities) — every checkpoint within the start of the run, so their "first interval" would be the whole run — are excluded from both first-interval contrasts and from the pooled hazard by band (Table S3 panel A; also in the sensitivities that produce bands) and stay in the per-km curve; `dropout_hazard_contrasts.csv` and `dropout_hazard_bands.csv` give, per contrast or band set, the events excluded for lacking such a checkpoint, and the contrasts file also the events in the contrast, with the start of the run observed and in the cohort (`dropout_hazard.txt` §1 lists them for the main analysis). `dropout_hazard_cohort_seasons.csv` persists the cohort events by season, with the first and last season, for the main analysis and the `years_le_2019` / `years_ge_2021` sensitivities (Table S3 labels the cohort up to 2019 by the seasons it covers). The per-km curve and its contrast exclude each event's terminal bin (the last 1-km bin of its run, which ends at or contains its finish).
4. **Continental mapping (athlete origin):** `country` text field (non-empty for 99.9% of starters and 99.6% of all analysed records), 369 variants mapped; short ambiguous codes (< 3 characters, 0.07%), including "us" and "US", left unmapped. Coverage persisted in `country_mapping.txt`.
5. **Race location:** structured metadata (`_race_locations.py`) — each race is mapped to its host country and the host country to a continent by `CONTINENT_MAP`, the athlete-origin mapping: 174 official series slugs (124 from v3 + 50 that entered with v4) and 331 secondary-source series mapped by hand, World Championships by year. 100% of starters mapped (`athlete_vs_venue.txt` §1), asserted at run time, together with a single host country per series across sources and years (a single continent per country holds by construction). See *Race location of secondary-source races* above.
6. **Regression standard errors:** clustered by event (`event_id`, one cluster per race-year) in `dnf_prediction.py` and `sensitivity_analyses.py` (combined model included); the odds ratios are those of the ordinary fit, CIs and p-values come from the clustered covariance; ORs, CIs and p persisted at full precision (`%.10g`), with the number of clusters.
7. **Rounding:** values printed in tables are rounded once, half up, from full-precision sources (`_common.fmt_half_up`; percentages of counts computed exactly by `pct_half_up`).
8. **Temporal trends:** the in-race DNF rate (in-race DNFs over starters) is the primary series of the trend tests, as in-race DNFs are the outcome of the regression and the event of the hazard; the total DNF rate is the secondary series and the finish-time DNF rate (finish-time DNFs over starters, their difference) a third one. The three are tested over each event type's seasons except 2020 (much reduced field) and the partial 2026 season: OLS slope on calendar year with a t-based 95% CI, R² and p; Mann-Kendall tau and p; Sen's slope against calendar year. The in-race series is also tested within the seasons up to 2009 and over 2010–2025 (2020 excluded) and pooled by period (in-race DNFs over starters summed over IRONMAN® 2002–2009, IRONMAN® 70.3 2004–2009, and both event types 2010–2019 and 2021–2025; 2020 and 2026 excluded). By season, the share of the starters and of the finish-time DNFs that come from the official source is persisted with its numerator and denominator. The annual counts, rates and shares (`temporal_trends_annual.csv`), the statistics (`temporal_trends_tests.csv`, column `seasons`) and the period rates (`temporal_trends_periods.csv`) are persisted at full precision; `temporal_trends.txt` shows them rounded (sections 1, 1b, 1d and 1e); section 1c gives, over the seasons of the trend tests (2020 and 2026 excluded), the highest and lowest annual rate of the in-race and total series and, for the season of the highest rate, the rate without its event with the highest in-race DNF share.
9. **Figure 1 population:** starters of known sex whose age-group code falls in one of the eleven age bins (professional categories and unrecognized codes fall outside them), with finish-time DNFs removed (`descriptives_age_gender.csv`, variant `excl_pro_finish_time`; the starters of known sex outside the bins are counted in `descriptives.txt` §3b). The regression starts from this population and further drops athletes of unknown continent of origin and starters at unmapped race locations.

## Reproducibility

All output files (txt and csv) start with a header: Python and package versions, the file read and the dataset version, seed, generation timestamp, the reference counts of the merged dataset file (constants of version 4.0.0, printed as such on any input; the counts of the file read are in `descriptives.txt`), the analysis-code fingerprint (sha256 of `notebooks/*.py` except `generate_figures.py`, `generate_tables.py` and `manuscript_number_audit.py`) and the analysed population (in the CSVs as `#` lines, read with `pd.read_csv(..., comment="#")`). Re-running the same code on the same platform produces identical outputs except the timestamp line. `manuscript_number_audit.py` is the check between `results/` and the manuscript (step 11).
